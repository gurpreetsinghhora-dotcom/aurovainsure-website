The daily reality

We know where the time goes

In most agencies, the property managers are also the admin team. Every renewal, repair and email lands on the same few desks, and the follow-up is what slips.

  • Certificate renewal dates showing red in the CRM, with nobody free to chase the landlord
  • Maintenance requests arriving by email, phone and portal, all needing logging and updating
  • Landlords and tenants waiting for replies while the inbox keeps growing
  • Records and documents spread across the CRM, shared drives and people's inboxes
  • Arrears and statements that need checking and chasing every month
  • Peak months when there simply aren't enough hours, and quiet months when hiring doesn't make sense
Renters' Rights Act

What's changed, and where admin support fits

The Renters' Rights Act 2025 changed how private tenancies work in England from 1 May 2026. Here is a plain summary of the main changes the government has announced. It isn't legal advice.

What changedWhat it can mean for your admin
Most private tenancies became assured periodic tenancies, and section 21 "no fault" evictions ended.Tenancy records, templates and landlord communications need updating, and evidence needs to be well organised.
Landlords and agents had to give existing tenants the government's Renters' Rights Act Information Sheet by 31 May 2026.Keeping a clear record of what was sent, to whom and when.
Rent can be increased only once a year, using the statutory process.Tracking when each increase is possible and preparing the paperwork for your review.
New rules on advertising, including a published asking rent and no bidding above it, plus rules on rent in advance and discrimination.Consistent processes for listings and applicant communications.
Later phases, including a private rented sector database and a landlord ombudsman, are being introduced over time.More information to gather, keep current and report, as each phase arrives.

Sources: GOV.UK: Renters' Rights Act, an overview for landlords and GOV.UK: renting out your property, guidance for landlords and letting agents. Always check the current official guidance.

Clear boundaries

What stays with you

We are an administration service. Your agency remains responsible to your landlords and tenants, and the decisions stay with your qualified people. That's how it should be.

Our team never carries out Right to Rent checks or holds tenants' identity documents.

We do not

  • Give legal advice, or prepare or serve possession notices or court documents
  • Make referencing or tenant selection decisions
  • Carry out Right to Rent checks
  • Inspect properties or certify safety or compliance
  • Handle or hold client money
  • Sign anything on your behalf
Questions from agents

Frequently asked questions

Which property software can you work with?

We work as named users inside the systems you already use, such as your property CRM, maintenance platform and email. We don't move your data into our own software. Tell us what you use and we'll confirm how access would work.

Who is responsible if something is missed?

We're responsible for doing the agreed tasks carefully and to the agreed standard, and our contract says so. Your agency stays responsible to your landlords and tenants, and decisions stay with you. We agree escalation rules up front, so anything urgent reaches the right person quickly.

Can you help with a backlog?

Yes. Many agencies start with a short clear-up project: bringing certificate dates, records and documents up to date. Then they move to routine monthly support. Tell us roughly how many properties are involved and what's behind.

Will my landlords and tenants know you're involved?

We work under your agency's name and processes, using your approved wording. How you describe the arrangement to your landlords is up to you, and we can help you update your privacy notice to reflect it.

Can we start small?

Yes. Most clients start with one or two clearly defined tasks, such as the compliance diary or maintenance logging, and add more once they're happy with the work.
